The case

Each lab committed one decision. Together they are the bet, the sequence, the team that owns it, the money, and what you learned building it.

Module 1
Product strategy
Where you play and how you win

Winning aspiration: Fable is the wellbeing app people keep paying for after they're already better, not the one that gets you through a crisis and is abandoned once it worked. It also helps prevent the next crisis by helping people spot their triggers.
Where to play: adults aged 25–45 who have already gone through a hard stretch, on iOS and Android, with lifecycle messaging and passive health signals. No children or teenagers.
How to win: Fable is a living, private playbook of each user's own history of what helped them through a hard stretch. It turns that past into readiness for the future, and competitors don't have that data.

The hard no

- Your one hard no: We will not build a community or social layer. - Safety and liability exposure. A social space for people coming out of distress will surface crisis disclosures in semi-public settings. That calls for moderation, escalation paths and clinical judgement, pulling us toward the clinical positioning we are not allowed to take. - Trust. Users came to us privately during a vulnerable time. A social layer changes the nature of that relationship.

Objective and key results

- Objective: Retain users after having a acute stretch to stay active - KR1: day-120-plus retention of the premium cohort rate increase 10% in 1 year - KR2: 50% of retained graduates maintain or improve their wellbeing score between day 90 and day 180. - KR3: Raise 12-month premium renewal among users who converted during their acute phase 10%

Module 2
Outcome roadmap
Now, next, and later

Now: Bet 1 · KR3 Renewal. If users save "what helped me" as their personal playbook while leaving a hard stretch, they'll see value beyond the crisis and renew at 12 months. _(Replaces: crisis mode flow. Capture the playbook at the moment of relief.)_; _to assign_; % of acute users with ≥1 saved entry; premium cancellations at 30 and 90 days compared with a control group
Next: Lifecycle state engine (acute, recovering, steady, re-entering). Needs Now Bets 1 + 2: states can only be defined once playbook and signal data exist.; _to assign_; Unlocked by Now evidence
Later: Bets, not commitments: playbook engine that predicts what each user needs next; research partnership publishing outcomes (needs 180-day cohort data from KR2); new content production only where gaps show up in the paths; research-updated techniques feed reviewed by clinical advisors; wearables beyond Apple Health and Health Connect; _to assign_; Right direction, not yet resourceable

Trade-off memo

I chose to sequence the three Now bets (playbook captured at relief, passive signals on both platforms, maintenance paths from existing content) first because each one keeps the need behind an original Rock but aims it at the part only Fable can own: the user's own history. We already win the crisis moment, and a content library is what incumbents do well, so the Rocks were reframed from features into bets:
- Crisis mode flow → Playbook captured at relief
- Apple Watch integration → Passive signals on both platforms
- Content library → Maintenance paths from existing content
I pushed out the lifecycle state engine, graduation and re-entry, lifecycle messaging, second-path re-entry and personal trigger awareness because each depends on evidence the Now bets have to produce first. States can't be defined without playbook and signal data, messaging bought before we know the moments just automates generic nudges, and trigger awareness needs enough history plus counsel sign-off to stay on the wellness side of the clinical line.
I cut a community or social layer entirely because it would surface crisis disclosures in semi-public settings, pulling us toward clinical positioning, and would change the private relationship users trusted us with. I also cut a premium tier with therapist-matching, because it isn't clear it would help retain users, and "Fable for Teams", because the effort would pull us away from fixing retention for the users we already have.

Module 5
Financial model
The business case

We will not launch therapist-matching this year. We don't have evidence it moves retention, and building a therapist network is a large, hard-to-reverse commitment. We know this removes a revenue and retention lever the CEO was counting on. Instead, next we'll ship the Apple Health integration and the content library, and analyse whether in one year we reach +10% day-120+ retention of the premium cohort and +10% 12-month premium renewal among users who converted during their acute phase. In parallel, Growth gets an early, split delivery of the AI check-in personalisation layer (about 2 weeks of engineering) to protect the Q3 goal of +15% Day-7 activation instead of ~7% without it.

Kill criterion

If day-120+ retention of the premium cohort does not reach +10% by 12 months after launch, we will revisit the retention bet before committing more to it.
If 12-month premium renewal among users who converted during their acute phase does not reach +10% by 12 months after launch, we will revisit the retention bet before committing more to it.
If data from guided content shows real demand for human support, we will bring a scoped therapist-matching pilot proposal to next year's roadmap.
